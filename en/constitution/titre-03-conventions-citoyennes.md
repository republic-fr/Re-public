# Title III — Citizens' conventions {#conventions}

## Chapter I — Common provisions

### Article 11 — Nature and list of the conventions {#nature-conventions-citoyennes #convention-secours}

Citizens' conventions are assemblies of citizens drawn by lot. They are the normal means of exercising sovereignty.

This Constitution establishes:

- the constituent convention, which amends the Constitution;

- the agenda-setting convention, which sets the multi-year strategic priorities;

- thematic conventions, which make the law on a specific subject;

- the annual ordinary convention, which adopts the budget and tax laws, ratifies major treaties and re-examines suspended texts;

- appointments conventions, which take part in the appointment of executive and administrative office-holders and external representatives;

- the emergency convention, which oversees crisis measures; its composition, the referral of matters to it, its powers and its duration are governed by Title [XII](#crises);

- territorial citizens' conventions, which exercise local deliberative functions.

### Article 12 — Composition and representativeness {#composition-representativite}

Conventions are composed of members of the civic body residing in France, drawn by lot under the conditions of Article [13](#eligibilite-participation). Their composition effectively reflects the population residing in France, according to criteria laid down by organic law which include at least sex, age, level of educational qualification, socio-professional category, type of area and geographical zone.

### Article 13 — Civic body and sortition {#eligibilite-participation}

The civic body comprises adult French nationals and adult foreign nationals who have been lawfully resident in France for at least five years. Its members sign petitions and vote in the referendums provided for by this Constitution. In this Constitution, the word ‘citizens’ refers to the members of the civic body, and titles of office refer to women and men alike.

Any member of the civic body residing in France may be drawn by lot. The draw is made from administrative population records, in particular tax and social security records, and not from electoral rolls, under conditions laid down by organic law which protect personal data. Persons placed under a legal protection regime or whose state of health prevents their participation benefit from adjustments or, failing that, are exempted.

Every sortition provided for by this Constitution is public and verifiable. The algorithm is published. The randomness comes from a public source, known only at the time of the draw. The Law Implementation Oversight Authority recomputes each draw and publishes the result. It audits each year the accuracy and completeness of the population records used.

A draw may be challenged before the Constitutional Council within fifteen days following the publication of its result. Once this period has elapsed, its regularity may no longer be called into question, except in the case of fraud established by a final criminal judgment. The convention concerned is then reconstituted, and the following annual ordinary convention confirms or repeals the acts it has adopted.

Sitting in a convention drawn by lot is a civic duty. Refusal is permitted only on serious grounds, in particular medical, family or force majeure, according to a procedure laid down by organic law, which also determines the consequences of an unjustified refusal. The person who refuses is replaced by a person drawn by lot from the same representativeness category. Refusal rates are published by category and audited each year by the Law Implementation Oversight Authority.

Each convention has substitutes, drawn by lot in the same manner as its members. A member may leave their convention during their term of office only on serious grounds. Pressure, a threat or a risk to their relatives constitutes a serious ground: it is examined confidentially by the Defender of Rights, who forwards the threat to the judicial authority. The departing member is replaced by a substitute.

### Article 14 — Status of convention members {#statut-conventionnel}

Every member of a convention enjoys a protective constitutional status, which includes in particular:

- full maintenance of their earned income, social benefits and pensions, without reduction or interruption of entitlements;

- a participation allowance, set by organic law, paid in addition to the maintenance of income;

- protection of their employment or activity throughout their participation;

- coverage of their travel, accommodation and meal expenses, as well as of their family constraints, in particular childcare and support for dependants;

- appropriate civic, legal and thematic training;

- material and administrative assistance;

- protection against pressure, threats, reprisals and conflicts of interest;

- the publicity of deliberations, the protection of individual speech and the secrecy of the vote.

Any member may request that their identity remain confidential during their term of office. Confidentiality is the rule for conventions dealing with defence or intelligence. Deliberations remain public, in a form that does not allow protected members to be identified.

For conventions whose members are automatically confidential, the draw is made from a record pseudonymised by the administration that holds it. The General Authority for Citizens' Conventions knows the members only by their pseudonym and their category. The Defender of Rights keeps the correspondence table, and its service sends out the summonses. Each summons gives rise to a pseudonymised acknowledgement of receipt, communicated to the General Authority and to the Law Implementation Oversight Authority. Failure to send a summons within the time limit constitutes serious misconduct within the meaning of Article [81](#jugement-fautes-graves).

For other conventions, only a restricted service of the General Authority has access to identities. Each consultation is recorded, and this register is audited each year by the Law Implementation Oversight Authority.

Any infringement of the status of convention members is punishable by law.

### Article 15 — Means of deliberation {#conditions-materielles-deliberation}

Each convention receives:

- a public briefing file presenting opposing views, accessible to all its members and to any person;

- pluralist expert opinions, including minority ones;

- public hearings, recorded and archived together with the public debates, available for consultation by all;

- neutral drafting assistance;

- accessibility of documents, exchanges and places of deliberation for all persons taking part;

- sufficient time for training, debate and deliberation, the minimum duration of which is laid down by organic law.

### Article 16 — Freedom and integrity of deliberation {#probite-deliberation}

Members deliberate freely. They are not bound by any imperative mandate and may not receive any instructions.

The rules of Title [XIV](#probite-deliberative) on the transparency of influence, integrity and the prevention of manipulation apply to conventions.

### Article 17 — Majorities, quorum and votes {#majorite-qualifiee}

Unless otherwise provided by this Constitution, a convention decides by an absolute majority of its members, in particular to adopt the law and the budget, for decisions of the agenda-setting convention and for appointments.

The qualified majority is two thirds of the members. It is reserved for exceptional decisions, in particular the confirmation of a suspended text, exceptional crisis measures and derogations from the priorities set by the agenda-setting convention.

Decisions on internal procedure and the organisation of work are taken by a simple majority of the members present.

A convention may validly deliberate only if a majority of its members is present. Failing that, it is reconvened within a period laid down by organic law, which may not exceed seven days.

Each vote leaves a trace allowing it to be verified, while respecting the secrecy of the vote. No member receives any document enabling them to prove how they voted.

### Article 18 — Common rules and organic laws {#application-autres-conventions}

This Chapter applies to all conventions provided for by this Constitution, including those governed by other Titles. Those Titles may adapt or supplement the common rules, but may never derogate from sortition, representativeness, the status of convention members or the integrity of deliberation.

Organic laws are adopted, by qualified majority, by the thematic convention competent for their subject matter. The authority whose organisation or functioning is at issue gives a public opinion, which does not bind the convention: no authority itself lays down the rules that govern it.

The Constitutional Council reviews every organic law before it enters into force. It verifies in particular that the law does not exceed what this Constitution refers to it and that it is consistent with the organic laws in force.

The organic laws necessary for the initial establishment of the institutions, in particular on sortition, the status of convention members and the authorities organising deliberation, are adopted by the constituent convention that adopts this Constitution and submitted to the same referendum.

## Chapter II — The agenda-setting convention

### Article 19 — Agenda-setting convention {#convention-programmation #programmation-saisines}

An agenda-setting convention of one hundred and fifty citizens drawn by lot is convened every five years.

It opens its work with a public review of the past period: the application of laws, the functioning of the institutions and citizen participation, drawing in particular on the reports of the Law Implementation Oversight Authority. It may derive from this review a proposal to amend the Constitution, under the conditions of Article [163](#revision-initiative).

It examines all the referrals recorded by the Civic Agenda Authority and sets in particular:

- the multi-year strategic priorities of public action;

- the legislative programme for the period and its allocation among thematic conventions;

- the main multi-year budgetary and tax guidelines, under the conditions of Title [XI](#finances-publiques).

It gives public reasons for its choices: the subjects selected, the subjects deferred and the reasons for these choices.

The duration of its work is laid down by organic law. It is dissolved at the end of that work.

## Chapter III — Thematic conventions

### Article 20 — Opening, composition and rapporteurs {#thematique-formation-objet}

Thematic conventions are opened by the Civic Agenda Authority, in application of the programme set by the agenda-setting convention or, by way of exception, in the cases provided for in Article [32](#apc-interdictions). Each comprises one hundred and fifty citizens drawn by lot and examines a specific subject, on which it may adopt one or more laws. It is dissolved at the end of its mission.

Before its dissolution, the convention appoints three rapporteurs from among its members who volunteer, by a simple majority of the members present. For five years, renewable once for five years if no thematic convention has been held on the same subject in the meantime, the rapporteurs may:

1° refer any deviation in the implementation of their convention's decisions to the annual ordinary convention, the Law Implementation Oversight Authority or the Court of Public Integrity;

2° be heard as of right by any subsequent thematic convention on a related subject;

3° contribute, at the request of the Civic Agenda Authority, to the training of a future convention.

They enjoy a status laid down by organic law, which protects them against pressure, provides them with a small secretariat and compensates them for the time they devote to their mission.

### Article 21 — Procedure and duration {#thematique-cycle-deliberatif #thematique-duree}

Each thematic convention proceeds as follows: training of its members, provision of a pluralist briefing file presenting opposing views, the public counter-expertise provided for in Title [XIV](#probite-deliberative), public hearings, protected internal deliberation, drafting with neutral assistance, adoption of the text, then dissolution.

The Civic Agenda Authority sets its duration at the opening, according to the complexity and volume of the subject, between a minimum and a maximum duration laid down by organic law. The convention may request a reasoned extension, under conditions laid down by organic law.

### Article 22 — Adoption of the law {#adoption-loi}

The thematic convention adopts the law by an absolute majority of its members.

With each law, it publishes a citizens' report, written in plain language: the questions examined, the options rejected and the reasons for rejecting them, the arguments of the majority and those of the minority, and the result of the votes.

Subject to scientific and constitutional review, the law enters into force on the date it specifies or, failing that, on the day following its official publication. The arrangements for publication are laid down by organic law.

### Article 23 — Re-examination of a suspended text {#reexamen-cas-suspension #cc-saisine-convention-secours}

Where the Constitutional Council or the ad hoc Scientific Council suspends a text, its decision classifies each objection as a procedural objection or a substantive objection. Every convention that adopts a text appoints three rapporteurs for this purpose, under the conditions of Article [20](#thematique-formation-objet).

In the case of a procedural objection, the rapporteurs may, within one month, withdraw or correct only the provisions concerned. They may not add any new provision. The Constitutional Council rules again within a period laid down by organic law. The text follows the procedure provided for substantive objections if the Council considers that the correction alters the general scheme of the text, if one third of the members of the original convention so request, or if the objection can be removed only by a new vote.

In the case of a substantive objection, the following annual ordinary convention re-examines the text in plenary session, after publicly hearing a rapporteur appointed by the authority that suspended the text and a rapporteur from the original convention. It may amend the text, which is then submitted again to review, abandon it or confirm it.

Confirmation requires a reasoned decision, adopted by a two-thirds majority of the members. The confirmed text enters into force and may no longer be suspended or repealed on the grounds of the objections examined. Confirmation is excluded where the objection concerns an absolute right referred to in Article [9](#droits-intangibles), where the Constitutional Council has held that the text abolishes a guaranteed right or impairs its substance, or where the objection concerns the competence of the convention, the adoption procedure or the organisation and powers of the institutions. Only a constitutional amendment, within the limits laid down in Title [XVI](#revision), may then remove the obstacle.

Where the text must enter into force before a fixed legal deadline, in particular the transposition deadline of a European Union act, the date on which a judicial decision takes effect or the beginning of the financial year, and the annual ordinary convention cannot rule in time, the Civic Agenda Authority refers the matter to the emergency convention. The emergency convention rules under the same conditions, after hearing the rapporteurs. If the suspended text was adopted by the emergency convention and that convention is still in operation, it re-examines the text itself.

## Chapter IV — The annual ordinary convention

### Article 24 — Purpose {#convention-ordinaire-objet}

The annual ordinary convention sits once a year, at the time of the examination of the budget, subject to the additional sessions provided for by this Constitution. Each year it adopts:

- the State budget and the annual tax laws, within the framework of the priorities set by the agenda-setting convention and under the conditions of Title [XI](#finances-publiques);

- the ratification of major treaties falling under Article [147](#traites-ratification);

- the re-examination of suspended texts, under the conditions of Article [23](#reexamen-cas-suspension);

- the ordinary judicial organisation, under the conditions of Article [99](#organisation-judiciaire);

- the examination of called-in decrees, under the conditions of Article [51](#subordination-loi).
