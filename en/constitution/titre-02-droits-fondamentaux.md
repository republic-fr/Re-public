# Title II — Fundamental rights {#droits}

### Article 8 — Declaration of Fundamental Rights {#declaration-droits-fondamentaux}

The Constitution is supplemented by a Declaration of Fundamental Rights, which is annexed to it and ranks above the law and the acts deriving from it. It is interpreted in the light of the international commitments of the Republic in the field of human rights.

The Declaration of Fundamental Rights is founded on the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen of 26 August 1789, the economic and social principles particularly necessary to our times set out in the Preamble to the Constitution of 27 October 1946, and the Charter for the Environment of 2004.

It is supplemented and clarified by the fundamental rights convention, a dedicated thematic convention, whose work may not reduce the substance of the fundamental human rights and freedoms already recognised, with the exception of the right to property, which is guaranteed in the terms of the following paragraph.

Property is guaranteed. Its use also serves the general interest, in particular social and environmental. No one may be deprived of it except on a ground of general interest defined by law, and subject to fair compensation. This paragraph replaces Article 17 of the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen of 1789.

### Article 9 — Inviolable rights {#droits-intangibles}

Inviolable rights comprise absolute rights and guaranteed rights.

The following are absolute and may not be subject to any restriction, derogation or suspension, on any ground whatsoever:

- human dignity;
- the prohibition of the death penalty, arbitrary executions and enforced disappearances;
- the prohibition of torture and of cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment;
- the prohibition of slavery, servitude and forced labour;
- the principle of legality of offences and penalties and the non-retroactivity of harsher criminal law.

The following are guaranteed:

- the right to physical and mental integrity;
- the freedom guaranteed to women to have recourse to voluntary termination of pregnancy, and the right to a dignified end of life;
- the equality of all before the law and the prohibition of discrimination;
- freedom of conscience, thought, opinion and religion;
- the presumption of innocence;
- the rights of the defence;
- the prohibition of arbitrary detention;
- the independence of the judiciary and the right to an effective judicial remedy.

The law may regulate the exercise of a guaranteed right, to the extent necessary and proportionate to an objective of general interest or to the protection of the rights of others. It may neither abolish it nor impair its substance. No guaranteed right may be suspended, even in time of crisis.

Any measure that restricts an absolute right, or that abolishes a guaranteed right, impairs its substance or regulates its exercise disproportionately, infringes an inviolable right.

### Article 10 — Principle of protective interpretation {#principe-interpretation-protectrice}

Where there is doubt as to the scope of a provision relating to rights and freedoms, it is interpreted in the manner most favourable to the person concerned.

Rules protecting vulnerable persons, in particular minors, persons placed under a legal protection regime and persons with disabilities, are interpreted in the manner most favourable to their protection.
