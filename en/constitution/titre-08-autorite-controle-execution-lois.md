# Title VIII — The Law Implementation Oversight Authority {#controle-execution}

## Chapter I — Nature and Organisation

### Article 63 — Nature and Structure {#acel-nature #acel-structure}

The Law Implementation Oversight Authority is an independent constitutional authority. It ensures the faithful, complete and diligent implementation of laws, of implementing acts and of all public policies. Through its public reports, it contributes to informing citizens.

It comprises:

- an annual citizens' plenary session, its sovereign deliberative body;

- a permanent technical service, responsible for investigations into the functioning of the administrations, for continuous monitoring and for the preparation of reports;

- the Defender of Rights, responsible for individual complaints by persons against the administrations and the bodies entrusted with a public service mission;

- a Standing Commission for the Oversight of Intelligence and the Armed Forces.

Decisions of substance and the general direction of the Authority fall exclusively to the plenary session, under whose general supervision the other components act. The Defender of Rights and the Standing Commission nevertheless have complete functional independence in the choice and investigation of their cases.

## Chapter II — The Annual Plenary Session

### Article 64 — Composition {#session-pleniere-composition}

The annual plenary session has one hundred and fifty citizens drawn by lot according to the representativeness criteria of Article [12](#composition-representativite). Its members are entirely renewed each year, with no continuity of persons. It sits for a period set by organic law, which may not be less than four weeks. Its members are protected by the status of convention members defined in Article [14](#statut-conventionnel).

### Article 65 — Powers {#session-pleniere-competences #session-pleniere-saisine}

The annual plenary session:

- examines and adopts the annual report prepared by the technical service;

- deliberates on the major dysfunctions identified and publicly sets out its criticisms and recommendations;

- may call to account any public administration, any constitutional authority and any public official, who are required to respond;

- refers the serious misconduct identified to the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability;

- may ask the Civic Agenda Authority to open corrective thematic conventions;

- may refer proposals for strategic reorientation to the next agenda-setting convention;

- sets the oversight programme of the technical service for the coming year;

- appoints the Prosecutor General for Public Accounts, examines their report and may disavow the actions of the technical service, under the conditions of Article [67](#procureur-general-comptes-publics);

- examines the annual reports of the Defender of Rights and of the Standing Commission; it may, within the limits compatible with secrecy, make public recommendations, ask the Commission for further investigations or refer serious misconduct brought to its attention to the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability;

- examines the annual activity reports of the Constitutional Council and of the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability; it may hear their members and make public recommendations, without ever calling into question a judicial decision.

The plenary session rules by an absolute majority of its members, unless otherwise provided by this Constitution.

Any person, any local authority, any convention and any constitutional authority may submit reasoned alerts to the Authority. The technical service handles them on a continuous basis; they feed into the annual report submitted to the plenary session.

## Chapter III — The Permanent Technical Service

### Article 66 — Court of Audit and Technical Service {#juridiction-financiere-service-technique}

The Court of Audit, a financial court whose status is laid down in Article [86](#independance-justice), shall forward on its own initiative to the annual plenary session all reports and findings liable to reveal serious misconduct or a structural dysfunction. It responds to requests for expertise from the plenary session and the technical service. It may refer matters to the Authority and to the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability according to the rules specific to each.

The permanent technical service of the Authority, distinct from the Court, comprises the non-judicial inspection and audit bodies. Its resources are set by law.

### Article 67 — Prosecutor General for Public Accounts {#procureur-general-comptes-publics #service-technique-reddition-comptes}

The technical service is headed by the Prosecutor General for Public Accounts, appointed by the annual plenary session from a shortlist drawn up after the prior integrity check of the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability. Their term of office is nine years, non-renewable.

They shall submit to each plenary session a detailed report on the investigations conducted, the referrals made, the requests addressed to the administration, their follow-up and the progress of the programme set by the previous session. The plenary session may, by a qualified majority, disavow the actions undertaken by the technical service during the year.

They may be removed by the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability, upon referral by the plenary session or under the other conditions of Title [IX](#juridiction-probite).

### Article 68 — Standing Powers {#service-technique-pouvoirs}

The technical service exercises the following powers on a continuous basis, in accordance with the programme set by the plenary session:

- to investigate the application of laws and the functioning of the administrations;

- to require the disclosure of any relevant document, subject to the secrets defined in Article [70](#secrets);

- to hear any public official;

- to ask the administration concerned to bring itself into compliance with the law;

- to refer matters to the competent courts, in particular the administrative courts, so that they order the implementation of a law that has been disregarded or annul an unlawful act;

- to refer matters to the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability in the event of serious misconduct attributable to a public official;

- to refer matters to the Constitutional Council in the event of serious doubt as to the conformity of an implementing act with the Constitution.

The Authority exercises only powers of request and referral. Sanctions fall exclusively to the courts and to the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability.

### Article 69 — Standing Commission for the Oversight of Intelligence and the Armed Forces {#controle-renseignement-armees}

The Standing Commission for the Oversight of Intelligence and the Armed Forces has twelve members, drawn by lot in a stratified manner from a public accreditation pool, according to the following rules:

- the pool comprises judges and prosecutors, members of the Council of State, lawyers specialising in civil liberties and criminal law, academics in law, political science, international relations or strategic studies, and former participants in the national session of the Institute for Advanced National Defence Studies or its equivalents;

- no professional body may account for more than one third of the members;

- judges and prosecutors who, in the ten years preceding their appointment, served in courts specialising in terrorism, intelligence or national security may not exceed one quarter of the members;

- persons who, in the preceding ten years, held positions within the intelligence services, the military staffs or the ministerial offices of Defence and the Interior are excluded;

- the accreditation conditions, set by organic law, guarantee disciplinary, methodological and intellectual pluralism, as well as diversity of professional backgrounds and of outlooks on civil liberties and national security.

The members' term of office is six years, non-renewable. Half of the Commission is renewed every three years. The Court of Public Integrity and Accountability checks the integrity of the members before their appointment and periodically during their term of office, according to the criteria of Article [80](#controle-prealable-probite). The Commission sits at least once a month. Its members are subject to an enhanced regime of integrity, security and secrecy set by organic law.

Under conditions that preserve strictly necessary secrets, the Commission has:

- full access to classified documents and information, programmes, contracts, operational activities and budgetary data of the intelligence services and the armed forces;

- the right to hear any civilian or military official, who may not invoke secrecy against it;

- the right to refer matters to the Constitutional Council, the Court of Public Integrity and the judicial authority;

- real-time information on any movement of the armed forces or the gendarmerie on national territory outside their ordinary missions, and on any deployment of intervention units of the internal security forces in the vicinity of the places mentioned in Article [114](#exclusion-regime-militaire-exception);

- the right to refer a matter immediately and automatically to the emergency convention and the Constitutional Council in the event of a suspected threat to the constitutional order.

Any infringement of its right of access constitutes serious misconduct within the meaning of Article [81](#jugement-fautes-graves). It reports each year to the plenary session, in a manner that protects legitimate secrets and makes structural findings public. Dissenting opinions are systematically published in its reports, under conditions compatible with secrecy.

### Article 70 — Secrets {#secrets}

Only the following may be protected by secrecy, within the meaning of this Constitution: military plans and operations in progress; the sources, methods and agents of the intelligence services; fallback positions and confidential elements of an ongoing international negotiation; information whose disclosure would endanger a protected person. No other information may be classified.

Every secret is of limited duration. It ends upon the conclusion or abandonment of the negotiation and, in other cases, at the latest twenty-five years after classification. Only the Commission may extend this duration, by a reasoned decision and for a fixed period.

Secrecy may not be invoked against the Commission, the courts, the Constitutional Council or the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability, which shall ensure its protection. Declassification is decided by the Commission or by the court seised, never by the authority that classified the information. No secret may cover an offence or an infringement of the rights guaranteed by this Constitution.

## Chapter IV — The Defender of Rights

### Article 71 — Mission and Independence {#defenseur-droits-nature-mission}

The Defender of Rights ensures respect for the rights and freedoms of persons by State administrations, local authorities, public establishments and bodies entrusted with a public service mission. The Defender combats discrimination, including in the private sector, in the areas defined by law. The Defender ensures the protection of the rights of the child.

Placed within the Oversight Authority, the Defender has complete functional independence. The Defender receives no instructions, including from the Prosecutor General for Public Accounts, in the choice and investigation of their cases.

### Article 72 — Appointment and Term of Office {#defenseur-droits-designation}

The Defender of Rights is appointed according to the following procedure:

- a public call for applications, according to criteria of competence set by organic law, in particular legal training, experience in human rights or public administration and the absence of conflicts of interest;

- a preselection by an ad hoc technical panel, drawn by lot from a public pool of experts in human rights and administration established by the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions;

- an integrity check of the preselected persons by the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability, according to the criteria of Article [80](#controle-prealable-probite);

- appointment by the annual plenary session, from among the admissible candidates, by an absolute majority of its members, in at most three rounds. Failing that, a new call for applications is opened.

The Defender's term of office is six years, non-renewable. The Defender enjoys security of tenure during their term of office. The Defender may be removed by the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability, upon referral by the plenary session or under the other conditions of Title [IX](#juridiction-probite).

### Article 73 — Referral {#defenseur-droits-saisine}

A matter may be referred to the Defender of Rights:

- by any person who considers themselves wronged by the functioning of an administration or of a body entrusted with a public service mission, whatever their nationality or administrative status, under the conditions set by law;

- by any person who is a victim of discrimination in the areas defined by law, including in the private sector;

- by any child, their legal representatives or any person who considers that the rights of the child have been infringed.

The Defender may also act on their own initiative in respect of a serious or structural failure to respect rights and freedoms.

### Article 74 — Powers {#defenseur-droits-pouvoirs}

To carry out their missions, the Defender of Rights may:

- conduct investigations, access any relevant document subject to the secrets defined in Article [70](#secrets), hold hearings and visit public and private establishments performing a public service mission, including places of deprivation of liberty;

- mediate between persons and the administrations;

- address reasoned public recommendations to the administrations and bodies concerned;

- apply to the courts for interim relief or on the merits in the event of a serious infringement of rights and freedoms;

- intervene before the courts, in particular by submitting observations or expertise;

- refer matters to the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability in the event of serious misconduct attributable to a public official;

- propose legislative or regulatory reforms or changes to administrative practices.

The administrations and bodies concerned shall respond to the Defender within the time limits set by organic law.

### Article 75 — Advisory College {#defenseur-droits-college}

The Defender of Rights is assisted by an advisory college of five to seven qualified persons, including at least one judge or prosecutor, one lawyer, one academic, one representative of human rights associations and one serving or retired mediator. They are drawn by lot, in a stratified manner, from public pools established by the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions on the proposal of the competent professional bodies, for a term of office of three years, not immediately renewable. The rules of the ad hoc Scientific Council on recusal, uniform remuneration and publication of dissenting opinions apply to them.

The college issues advisory opinions on cases of structural importance and on the reports of the Defender of Rights. It has no decision-making power of its own.

### Article 76 — Accountability {#defenseur-droits-reddition-comptes}

The Defender of Rights shall submit to each annual plenary session a detailed report on their activity: referrals received, investigations conducted, mediations undertaken, recommendations made and their follow-up, and referrals to the courts and to the Court of Public Integrity. The Defender also publishes thematic reports on the structural issues they identify.

The plenary session examines these reports. It may make public recommendations, ask the Civic Agenda Authority to open corrective thematic conventions, or refer proposals for strategic reorientation to the next agenda-setting convention.
