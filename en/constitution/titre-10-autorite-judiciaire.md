# Title X — On the Judicial Authority and the Administrative Courts {#autorite-judiciaire}

## Chapter I — General principles

### Article 86 — Independence of justice and the courts {#independance-justice}

The judicial authority is independent. No public authority may give instructions to the courts in the exercise of their judicial functions, or intervene in pending cases. The independence of justice is a component of the constitutional identity of the Republic, within the meaning of Article [154](#identite-constitutionnelle-republique), and a right guaranteed by Article [9](#droits-intangibles).

The administrative courts, comprising the administrative tribunals, the administrative courts of appeal and the Council of State, adjudicate disputes between persons and the administration and review the legality of executive acts. They are independent under the same conditions as the judicial authority. The Council of State exercises exclusively judicial functions.

The Court of Audit is an independent financial court. It rules on the accounts of public accountants, gives its opinion on the regularity, fairness and effectiveness of public management, and exercises the judicial powers conferred on it by law. Its judges are irremovable. Their status, the conditions of their appointment and their discipline are laid down by organic law and safeguarded by the High Council of the Judiciary, in its section with jurisdiction over the administrative courts. Its cooperation with the Law Implementation Oversight Authority is governed by Article [66](#juridiction-financiere-service-technique). The other provisions of this Title apply to its judges only where they expressly so provide.

In this Title, the provisions relating to members of the judiciary apply to members of the administrative courts, unless otherwise provided.

### Article 87 — Essential guarantees {#garanties-essentielles}

Everyone has the right to an independent and impartial judge, to a fair and adversarial procedure, to the rights of the defence, to an effective remedy and to proceedings within a reasonable time. The equality of all persons before the courts is guaranteed. No one may be arbitrarily detained. No one may be sentenced to death.

### Article 88 — Status of members of the judiciary {#statut-magistrats #statut-magistrats-normes}

Judges and prosecutors are irremovable. They may be transferred or promoted only with their consent or by reasoned decision of the High Council of the Judiciary.

Any attempt to exert influence, direct or indirect, over a member of the judiciary, by a public authority, an interest representative or any other person, constitutes an infringement of the independence of justice, punishable by law. Members of the judiciary shall report any pressure to which they are subjected. The report is forwarded to the High Council of the Judiciary and, where it concerns a public office-holder, to the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability.

The status of members of the judiciary, which covers the rules on recruitment, training, career, professional obligations, rights, remuneration and professional ethics, is laid down by the law adopted in a dedicated thematic convention, after a reasoned and public opinion of the High Council of the Judiciary. The Council may refer to the Civic Agenda Authority a reasoned proposal for the opening of such a convention.

### Article 89 — Public prosecution service {#independance-parquet}

The public prosecution service is fully independent of the executive power. No minister or executive authority may give instructions, general or individual, to prosecutors.

Prosecutors are appointed, promoted and disciplined by the High Council of the Judiciary, under the same conditions of independence as judges.

Criminal justice policy, in its priorities and general guidelines, is laid down exclusively by the law adopted in a citizens' convention. The Prosecutor General at the Court of Cassation submits each year to the plenary session of the Law Implementation Oversight Authority a report on the conduct of criminal justice policy and the functioning of the public prosecution service.

## Chapter II — The High Council of the Judiciary

### Article 90 — Role and sections {#csm-nature-role #csm-formations}

The High Council of the Judiciary is an independent constitutional institution. It safeguards the independence of the judicial authority by overseeing the appointments, careers, discipline and professional ethics of members of the judiciary.

It comprises three sections, with jurisdiction respectively over judges, prosecutors and members of the administrative courts. A plenary section, composed in a manner laid down by organic law, deals with matters common to the three bodies and gives opinions on proposed reforms of the judicial organisation or of the status of members of the judiciary.

### Article 91 — Composition {#csm-composition}

Each section has twelve members:

- seven members of the judiciary from the body concerned (judges, prosecutors or administrative courts);

- one member of the judiciary from another body, who provides liaison between the sections;

- one lawyer;

- two academics specialising in law;

- one qualified person from civil society.

When ruling on disciplinary matters, the section has twelve members: five members of the judiciary from the body concerned, drawn by lot from among the seven, the lawyer, the two academics, the qualified person and three citizens drawn by lot for each case. Members of the judiciary are thus in the minority.

### Article 92 — Appointment of members {#csm-designation-membres}

The judicial members are elected by their peers, under the conditions laid down by organic law, with due regard to the representation of the different levels of court and the different types of function.

The non-judicial members are drawn by lot, on a stratified basis, from public pools constituted according to objective, verifiable criteria laid down by organic law. The following are automatically entered:

- in the lawyers' pool, all lawyers registered with a French bar, whose lists are kept by the regional bar councils;

- in the academics' pool, all tenured university professors of law, whose lists are kept by the law faculties;

- in the qualified persons' pool, persons who meet objective criteria laid down by organic law, in particular recognised experience in the defence of rights, oversight of public administration, institutional ethics or mediation; the lists are kept by the civil society institutions designated by organic law.

The common principles governing the pools under Article [132](#vivier-public-experts) apply. The Court of Public Integrity and Accountability reviews the integrity of members before their appointment, according to the criteria of Article [80](#controle-prealable-probite).

### Article 93 — Term of office and presidency {#csm-duree-mandat #csm-presidence}

The term of office of members is six years and is not renewable. Half of the Council is renewed every three years. Members may be removed during their term of office by the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability, under the conditions of Title [IX](#juridiction-probite).

The sections are presided over respectively by the First President of the Court of Cassation, by the Prosecutor General at the Court of Cassation and by the President of the Council of State. These three office-holders are appointed by the plenary section of the Council, by qualified majority, from among the members of the court concerned with the most extensive experience, after a prior integrity check by the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability. Members of the plenary section whose succession is at issue do not take part in the vote.

The plenary section is presided over in rotation by the presidents of the three sections, in a manner laid down by organic law.

## Chapter III — Powers of the High Council of the Judiciary

### Article 94 — Appointments {#csm-nominations}

Each section proposes appointments to the highest posts in its body:

- for judges, in particular to the Court of Cassation, to the first presidencies of the courts of appeal and to the presidencies of the first-instance courts;

- for prosecutors, in particular to the office of the Prosecutor General at the Court of Cassation and to the posts of prosecutor general and public prosecutor;

- for the administrative courts, to the Council of State and to the presidencies of the administrative courts of appeal and of the administrative tribunals.

Other members of the judiciary are appointed on its binding opinion. No appointment, including to the public prosecution service, may be made without the binding approval of the High Council of the Judiciary.

### Article 95 — Discipline and referral by litigants {#csm-discipline #csm-saisine-justiciables}

The High Council of the Judiciary exercises disciplinary authority over judges and prosecutors and over members of the administrative courts, each section sitting as the disciplinary council for its own body. Proceedings are adversarial. Decisions are reasoned and public, except where strictly justified by the protection of third parties. They are subject to judicial appeal before a dedicated section of the Council, composed of members who did not sit at first instance, with the composition provided for disciplinary matters in Article [91](#csm-composition).

Any litigant may refer to the Council the conduct of a member of the judiciary in the exercise of their functions, where that conduct may constitute a breach of professional or ethical obligations. A screening committee, composed of members of the competent section, dismisses manifestly unfounded complaints, challenges to judicial decisions and criticisms that fall solely within the ordinary means of appeal. Admissible referrals are examined by the competent disciplinary section. This referral does not deprive the litigant of the ordinary judicial remedies.

### Article 96 — Professional ethics and opinions {#csm-deontologie #csm-avis-reformes}

The High Council of the Judiciary oversees the professional ethics of members of the judiciary. It draws up a compendium of ethical obligations, which it updates regularly. It may issue public opinions and recommendations on any matter relating to the conduct of members of the judiciary.

In plenary section, it issues a reasoned and public opinion on any proposed reform of the status of members of the judiciary or of the judicial organisation, before its examination by the competent convention. The opinion is not binding on the convention, which shall state its reasons if it departs from it.

### Article 97 — Relationship with the Court of Public Integrity {#repartition-probite-judiciaire}

The High Council of the Judiciary has jurisdiction over the careers of members of the judiciary, in particular appointments, promotions, transfers and internal professional discipline.

The Court of Public Integrity and Accountability adjudicates serious misconduct committed by members of the judiciary in the exercise of their functions, within the meaning of Article [81](#jugement-fautes-graves), in particular infringements of fundamental rights, abuses of power, corruption and serious conflicts of interest. It also exercises over them the integrity review provided for in Article [80](#controle-prealable-probite).

The two institutions may refer to each other facts brought to their attention.

## Chapter IV — Democratic oversight and judicial organisation

### Article 98 — Democratic oversight {#csm-controle-annuel #csm-examen-quinquennal}

The High Council of the Judiciary submits each year to the Law Implementation Oversight Authority a public activity report on appointments, disciplinary decisions, ethics case law, the handling of referrals by litigants and the general functioning of the judiciary.

The plenary session of the Oversight Authority examines this report. It may question the members of the Council, request clarification, make public recommendations, refer to the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability serious misconduct found on the part of a member of the Council or of a member of the judiciary, and request the Civic Agenda Authority to open a corrective thematic convention. It examines collective complaints from litigants about the general functioning of justice that do not fall within the individual referrals under Article [95](#csm-discipline).

Every five years, the Council submits to the agenda-setting convention a strategic report on the state of justice, developments in the judiciary, structural issues and reform needs. The agenda-setting convention incorporates these elements into its guidelines and may request the opening of thematic conventions on the status of members of the judiciary or the judicial organisation.

### Article 99 — Judicial organisation {#organisation-judiciaire}

Ordinary judicial organisation, which covers adjustments to the map of courts, the creation and abolition of chambers and changes to the jurisdiction of courts, is laid down by the law adopted in the annual ordinary convention, after a reasoned and public opinion of the High Council of the Judiciary.

Major overhauls of the judicial organisation, in particular substantial changes to the map of courts and the creation or abolition of categories of courts, fall within the remit of a dedicated thematic convention.

In the event of a dispute over the nature of a judicial organisation measure, any constitutional authority may refer the matter to the Constitutional Council in order to determine the competent convention.

### Article 100 — Organic law {#loi-organique-judiciaire}

An organic law specifies the application of this Title, in particular the detailed composition, organisation and functioning of the High Council of the Judiciary, the disciplinary procedure, the conditions for referral by litigants and the procedure for appointing the First President of the Court of Cassation, the Prosecutor General at that Court and the President of the Council of State.
