# Title V — The Constitutional Council {#conseil-constitutionnel}

## Chapter I — Nature, composition and status

### Article 35 — Nature {#cc-nature}

The Constitutional Council is an independent court. It reviews the conformity of laws, implementing acts and treaties with this Constitution and with the Declaration of Fundamental Rights. The review of treaties is governed by Article [150](#traites-controle-conformite).

### Article 36 — Composition {#cc-composition}

The Constitutional Council is composed of members of the judiciary and of lawyers with equivalent judicial training. The organic law shall set the number of its members, which may not be fewer than nine.

The members are drawn by lot, on a stratified basis, from a permanent public accreditation pool, constituted according to objective, verifiable criteria laid down by the organic law. The draw guarantees diversity of professional backgrounds.

The following are automatically entered in the pool:

- serving or honorary judges and prosecutors who have performed judicial duties on the bench or in the public prosecution service;

- lawyers admitted to a French bar with proven practice in constitutional or administrative litigation or litigation concerning fundamental freedoms;

- tenured university professors in a relevant legal discipline.

The pool is stratified by fields of specialisation according to a nomenclature laid down by the organic law. A person may appear in several fields according to their proven qualifications. The lists are kept up to date by the High Council of the Judiciary for members of the judiciary, by the bar councils for lawyers and by the universities for professors.

The organic law shall lay down the conditions of accreditation, in particular minimum judicial experience, the quality of legal work, diversity of experience and the absence of conflicts of interest. The principles common to the pools, in particular appeal against a refusal of entry and review of plurality, are laid down in Article [132](#vivier-public-experts).

### Article 37 — Term of office {#cc-duree-mandat}

Members serve a nine-year term, which is not renewable. One third of the Council is renewed every three years, in order to ensure continuity of case law and diversity of perspectives. In the event of a vacancy, a new member is drawn by lot from the pool, in the same field of specialisation, for the remainder of the term.

### Article 38 — Status and independence {#cc-statut-independance}

Members are subject to strict obligations of independence, impartiality and integrity. During their term of office, they may not hold any other public office, engage in any professional activity likely to create a conflict of interest, or hold any party leadership office.

They may not be removed from office, except by decision of the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability taken under the conditions of Title [IX](#juridiction-probite) and confirmed by the Constitutional Council sitting without the member concerned.

The organic law shall lay down their remuneration, their protection and their conditions of service, so as to guarantee their effective independence.

## Chapter II — Review of laws

### Article 39 — Automatic prior review of laws {#controle-prealable-automatique-lois}

Every law adopted by a citizens' convention is automatically referred to the Constitutional Council before it enters into force.

Constitutional review and the scientific review provided for in Title [VI](#controle-scientifique) are carried out in parallel. Each is completed within one month of the adoption of the text. This time limit may be extended once by one month by reasoned decision, then a second time by one month in the event of an exceptional influx of referrals recorded by public decision. Prior reviews take priority over any other case. On expiry of these time limits, the text enters into force; it remains subject to subsequent review.

Before ordering a suspension, the Council shall hear in public a rapporteur appointed by the convention that adopted the text, as well as any authority it considers it useful to hear.

### Article 40 — Subsequent review {#cc-saisine-posteriori}

Once the law is in force, any natural or legal person with standing may challenge it before the Constitutional Council. The Council shall dismiss of its own motion, by reasoned decision, referrals that are manifestly inadmissible, in particular for lack of standing, where the question has already been decided or where the question is manifestly unfounded.

A matter may also be referred to the Council, without any admissibility filter, by:

- any current or past citizens' convention, concerning a text or an implementing act that it adopted or that concerns it;

- the Civic Agenda Authority, the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions and the Law Implementation Oversight Authority;

- the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability;

- any minister, concerning any law or any implementing act affecting their field;

- any external relations representative, concerning any treaty before its final ratification.

Any party to proceedings before a court may argue that a legislative provision applicable to the dispute infringes the Constitution. If the question is serious and has not already been decided, the court shall refer it to the Constitutional Council and stay proceedings, except in cases of urgency or where a person is deprived of liberty. The Council shall rule within a time limit laid down by the organic law.

### Article 41 — Review of implementing acts {#controle-actes-execution}

The administrative courts review the legality of implementing acts, such as decrees, orders and circulars issued for the application of the law, under the conditions laid down in Article [86](#independance-justice).

Where a challenge before the administrative courts raises a serious question as to the conformity of an implementing act with the Constitution, the court shall refer it to the Constitutional Council. The authorities and conventions mentioned in the second paragraph of Article [40](#cc-saisine-posteriori) may also refer an implementing act directly to the Council. The Council rules only on the conformity of the act with the Constitution.

## Chapter III — Scope and effects of review

### Article 42 — Scope of review {#cc-etendue-controle}

The Constitutional Council reviews in particular:

- conformity with the Constitution and with the Declaration of Fundamental Rights;

- compliance with constitutional procedures, in particular the competence of the body that adopted the text and the required majority;

- compliance with the division of powers between the national level and the territorial levels;

- respect for the inviolable rights guaranteed in Article [9](#droits-intangibles);

- the proportionality of restrictions on public freedoms.

### Article 43 — Effect of decisions {#effet-controle-prealable #effet-controle-posteriori}

Where the Council declares a text non-compliant during prior review, it suspends its entry into force. Its decision specifies the objections and the articles concerned.

Where it declares a law or an implementing act non-compliant after its entry into force, it determines the effects of its decision over time. It may order immediate repeal, repeal deferred to a date it sets, or any transitional measure necessary for legal certainty and the protection of acquired rights.

In both cases, the text is re-examined under the conditions laid down in Article [23](#reexamen-cas-suspension).

### Article 44 — Authority of decisions {#cc-autorite-decisions}

The decisions of the Constitutional Council are reasoned and public, and are accompanied by any dissenting opinions. They are binding on public authorities and on all administrative and judicial authorities.

The Council shall present each year a public activity report to the plenary session of the Law Implementation Oversight Authority, under the conditions laid down in Article [65](#session-pleniere-competences).
