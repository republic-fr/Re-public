# Title IX — On the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability {#juridiction-probite}

## Chapter I — Nature and composition

### Article 77 — Nature {#juridiction-probite-nature}

The Court of Public Integrity and Accountability is an independent constitutional court. It reviews the integrity of public office-holders before their appointment and periodically during their term of office, and adjudicates serious misconduct committed in the exercise of public functions.

### Article 78 — Composition {#juridiction-probite-composition}

The Court has fifteen members, exclusively members of the judiciary or legal professionals with equivalent judicial training, having particular expertise in criminal law, disciplinary law, administrative law or the review of public integrity.

The members are drawn by lot, on a stratified basis, from a permanent public accreditation pool, constituted according to objective, verifiable criteria laid down by organic law. The draw ensures diversity of professional backgrounds and fields of specialisation.

The following are automatically entered in the pool:

- serving and honorary judges and prosecutors who have held judicial office on the bench or in the public prosecution service;

- lawyers registered with a French bar and practising mainly in one of the specialities mentioned in the first paragraph;

- tenured university professors in a relevant legal discipline;

- senior councillors (conseillers maîtres) of the Court of Audit and judges of the financial courts, serving or honorary.

The pool is stratified by field of specialisation according to a classification laid down by organic law. A person may be listed in several fields according to their certified qualifications. The lists are kept up to date by the High Council of the Judiciary for judges and prosecutors, by the bar councils for lawyers, by the universities for professors and by their parent bodies for other legal professionals. The common principles governing the pools under Article [132](#vivier-public-experts) apply.

Entry in the pool requires compliance with the rules of independence, impartiality and integrity laid down by organic law. The High Council of the Judiciary may remove from the pool, by reasoned decision, any person who no longer complies with them. That decision may be appealed before the Court itself, sitting in a panel separate from any panel of which the person concerned might be a member.

### Article 79 — Term of office, status and independence {#juridiction-probite-duree-mandat #juridiction-probite-statut}

The term of office of members is nine years and is not renewable. One third of the Court is renewed every three years. In the event of a vacancy, a new member is drawn by lot from the pool, in the same field of specialisation, for the remainder of the term.

Membership is absolutely incompatible with any ministerial office, any external representation function and any office in another constitutional authority. A cooling-off period of three years is required between holding any of these offices and a term as a member, and vice versa.

The remuneration of members is uniform and independent of reputation or prior experience. Dissenting opinions are published. The integrity of members is reviewed, before their appointment and periodically during their term of office, by a specific panel of the Court that does not include the members concerned, under conditions laid down by organic law.

The Court presents an annual public activity report to the plenary session of the Law Implementation Oversight Authority, under the conditions laid down in Article [65](#session-pleniere-competences).

## Chapter II — Functions and scope

### Article 80 — Integrity review {#controle-prealable-probite #controle-periodique-integrite}

Before any appointment to an office subject to its review, the Court verifies, for each candidate:

- financial integrity, on the basis of a comprehensive and public declaration of assets and of the absence of serious tax penalties or convictions for embezzlement, corruption or fraud;

- integrity of interests, on the basis of a comprehensive and public declaration of interests and of the absence of active ties incompatible with the office;

- criminal integrity, according to a compatibility grid laid down by organic law;

- constitutional integrity, assessed exclusively in the light of acts that have resulted in a final conviction: violence, public incitement to violence, attacks on the constitutional institutions or on the inviolable rights guaranteed in Article [9](#droits-intangibles).

The opinions expressed by a candidate may under no circumstances form the basis of a decision of inadmissibility. The Court draws up the list of admissible candidates and transmits it to the appointments convention or to the competent appointing authority.

During the term of office, the Court periodically reviews the integrity of the public office-holders subject to its review, in particular on the basis of their updated declarations of assets and interests. In the event of a serious breach, this review may lead to accountability proceedings.

### Article 81 — Serious misconduct {#jugement-fautes-graves}

The Court adjudicates serious misconduct committed in the exercise of public functions. Serious misconduct includes in particular:

- deliberate failure to implement a law;

- distortion of the intent of a law, that is, its application in a manner manifestly contrary to the objectives that emerge from its text and from the report published by the convention;

- concealment of decisive information;

- abuse, excess or misuse of power;

- infringement of fundamental rights in the exercise of office;

- unlawful use of means of coercion or surveillance;

- corruption, serious conflict of interest and illicit enrichment;

- serious breach of transparency obligations;

- infringement of the independence of another constitutional authority;

- breach of the constitutional oath taken pursuant to Article [50](#nature-administration-execution).

### Article 82 — Persons subject to its review {#juridiction-probite-perimetre}

The following are subject to the review of the Court, within the meaning of this Constitution:

- ministers and members of the executive administration;

- external relations representatives;

- members of the Constitutional Council;

- members of the Court itself, tried by a specific panel that does not include the members concerned and is supplemented by three members of the Constitutional Council drawn by lot;

- members of the Law Implementation Oversight Authority and the Prosecutor General for Public Accounts;

- members of the Civic Agenda Authority and the senior officials of the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions;

- members of the High Council of the Judiciary, and members of the judiciary under the conditions laid down in Article [97](#repartition-probite-judiciaire);

- the Defender of Rights;

- members of the Independent Regulatory Authority for Democratic Life;

- general officers of the armed forces;

- local executives, on referral by the competent territorial conventions.

With the exception of members of the Civic Agenda Authority, who exercise an autonomous and lasting power, members of citizens' conventions fall within the jurisdiction of the Court only in cases of clearly established criminal misconduct.

## Chapter III — Referral, procedure and penalties

### Article 83 — Referral {#juridiction-probite-saisine}

The prior integrity check is systematic for any appointment to an office subject to the review of the Court. Matters are referred to the Court by the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions or by the competent appointing authority.

Accountability review is opened on referral by:

- the Law Implementation Oversight Authority;

- the Constitutional Council;

- the emergency convention;

- any constitutional authority, for the office-holders whose action it oversees;

- any citizens' convention, for the office-holders it has appointed or whose action it oversees;

- any person demonstrating standing to act, subject to the admissibility filter defined in the following paragraph.

The Court dismisses of its own motion, by reasoned decision, manifestly inadmissible referrals, in particular for lack of standing, where the question has already been decided or where the allegation is manifestly unfounded.

Any person may also refer a matter to the Court where new facts come to light affecting the integrity of a public office-holder which were not known or had not been disclosed at the time of the appointment or of the last periodic review.

### Article 84 — Procedure and appeals {#juridiction-probite-procedure}

Proceedings are adversarial. The office-holder concerned may defend themselves, submit documents and be assisted by counsel.

Hearings are public, except where strictly justified by the protection of third parties or by a secret defined in Article [70](#secrets). Decisions are reasoned, public and accompanied by dissenting opinions. The Court rules within a reasonable time limit laid down by organic law.

Its decisions may be appealed only before an appeal panel of the Court, composed of members who did not sit at first instance. Decisions declaring a candidacy inadmissible and decisions of removal from office may also be brought before the Constitutional Council.

### Article 85 — Penalties {#juridiction-probite-sanctions}

Depending on the seriousness of the breaches, the Court may impose:

- a reasoned warning, for minor breaches that do not justify removal from office;

- removal from office;

- temporary disqualification, for two to ten years, from holding any public office or any office subject to its review;

- permanent disqualification from holding such offices, for particularly serious misconduct;

- additional measures, in particular publication of the reasoned decision;

- referral to the criminal courts of facts that may constitute a criminal offence.
