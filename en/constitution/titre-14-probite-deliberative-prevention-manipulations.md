# Title XIV — Deliberative integrity and the prevention of manipulation {#probite-deliberative}

## Chapter I — General principles

### Article 129 — Integrity of deliberation and transparency of influence {#probite-deliberation-citoyenne #obligation-generale-transparence-influences}

The quality of deliberation is a constitutional condition of the legitimacy of the law. The Republic protects the formation of the general will against manipulation, capture, corruption, disinformation and information asymmetries. The provisions of this Title supplement the guarantees laid down by the other Titles of the Constitution.

Any influence exerted on a citizens' convention, on a constitutional authority or on the executive administration shall be declared, recorded and made public in a manner that ensures it is intelligible to citizens.

This obligation covers in particular:

- meetings between public office-holders and interest representatives;

- notes, contributions and studies added to the files;

- indirect funding, in particular sponsored studies, funded conferences, travel and receptions;

- oral statements made in the course of hearings;

- positions publicly defended in connection with matters under deliberation.

## Chapter II — Public register of influence

### Article 130 — Public register {#registre-public-influences}

A permanent public register of influence is kept by the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions. It records, for each citizens' convention and for each constitutional authority:

- hearings, the identity, capacity and affiliation of those heard, their duration and the subjects discussed;

- written contributions received;

- individual meetings between members and interest representatives;

- external notes added to the file;

- known indirect funding;

- declarations of interests of the experts heard.

The register is kept under conditions of accessibility, intelligibility and searchability laid down by organic law. The Law Implementation Oversight Authority reviews the regularity of its keeping.

### Article 131 — Prohibitions and sanctions {#influences-interdictions-sanctions}

The following are prohibited and sanctioned:

- undeclared contacts between interest representatives and public office-holders or members of conventions;

- pressure, threats, advantages or promises directed at a member of a convention or of an authority;

- corruption, illicit enrichment and serious conflicts of interest;

- the deliberate withholding of decisive information;

- organised information manipulation, within the meaning of Article [137](#atteinte-souverainete-populaire);

- the illicit funding of parties, political groups or campaigns;

- fraud in citizens' initiative petitions or referendum campaigns;

- coordinated harassment targeting persons or institutions because of their participation in democratic life.

For public office-holders, the matter is referred to the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability under the conditions laid down in Title [IX](#juridiction-probite).

For members of citizens' conventions, the convention may, by a qualified majority, expel one of its members for proven serious misconduct, by a reasoned decision. This decision may be appealed before the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability.

Any corruption, attempted corruption, pressure, threat, reprisal, intimidation, collection or exploitation of personal information, disclosure of the identity of a member whose identity is protected, or disinformation campaign using the fraudulent methods defined in Article [137](#atteinte-souverainete-populaire), directed against a member of a citizens' convention, of an expert panel, of the ad hoc Scientific Council, of the Court of Public Integrity or of the Constitutional Council, or against their relatives, in connection with their duties, constitutes an offence against popular sovereignty, a classification of a constitutional nature.

These offences are subject to an aggravated criminal regime, an extended limitation period and universal jurisdiction of the French courts where they are committed from abroad or by foreign persons or entities. The penalties may not be lower than those laid down for the corruption of persons holding public authority. The liability of legal persons may entail their dissolution.

For interest representatives and persons exerting undeclared influence, the law adopted in convention defines the offences and the applicable criminal penalties.

## Chapter III — Pluralism of expertise

### Article 132 — Public pool of experts {#vivier-public-experts}

The General Authority for Citizens' Conventions establishes and keeps up to date a public pool of experts by field, for purposes including the preparation of convention files and the selection of hearings.

This pool is distinct from the pool of the ad hoc Scientific Council provided for in Article [46](#vivier-scientifique-designation) and from the pool of the ministerial technical panel provided for in Article [55](#jury-technique-ministres). The conditions of accreditation, based on objective criteria of competence, experience, disciplinary and methodological diversity and absence of conflicts of interest, are laid down by organic law.

Any refusal of accreditation shall state reasons and may be appealed before the Civic Agenda Authority, whose decision may be brought before the Constitutional Council.

Every public accreditation pool established pursuant to this Constitution is open in accordance with the following principles:

- two routes of access: each candidate may seek accreditation on the proposal of a professional, academic or learned body, or by personal application supported by documents attesting the required competences, without either route taking precedence;

- objective and public accreditation criteria, based on competence and integrity, to the exclusion of any criterion of prominence or of dominant institutional background;

- public objectives of social, territorial, methodological and school-of-thought diversity, compliance with which is the subject of an annual report published by the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions and of a five-yearly audit;

- public statement of reasons for any refusal of accreditation, which may be appealed before the Civic Agenda Authority, then before the Constitutional Council;

- keeping of the lists up to date on a purely administrative basis, without any power of proposal, selection, ranking or exclusion;

- review of the effective plurality of the pool by the Civic Agenda Authority, according to the indicators provided for in Article [31](#apc-missions).

These principles apply without prejudice to the refusals of accreditation and removals provided for by this Constitution.

These principles apply to the pools provided for in Articles [36](#cc-composition), [46](#vivier-scientifique-designation), [55](#jury-technique-ministres), [69](#controle-renseignement-armees), [72](#defenseur-droits-designation), [75](#defenseur-droits-college), [78](#juridiction-probite-composition), [92](#csm-designation-membres), [132](#vivier-public-experts) and [140](#autorite-regulation-vie-democratique).

### Article 133 — Independent expert panel {#jury-experts-independants #jury-experts-mission #jury-experts-independance}

For each thematic convention and for the agenda-setting convention, an independent expert panel is formed to prepare the file and propose hearings.

The members of the panel are appointed by stratified sortition from the pool provided for in Article [132](#vivier-public-experts). The sortition takes place automatically as soon as the convention is opened by the Civic Agenda Authority, according to stratification rules guaranteeing disciplinary, methodological and school-of-thought pluralism, laid down by organic law. No authority chooses the members of the panel.

The panel is dissolved upon delivery of its file to the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions.

The independent expert panel:

- prepares the basic file: state of the applicable law, available data, issues at stake, opposing arguments, points of controversy, outlook;

- proposes a first list of experts to be heard, ensuring disciplinary, methodological and school-of-thought pluralism, as well as the representation of minority currents and alternative approaches.

The panel has no power of political direction. Its mission is strictly one of adversarial and pluralist synthesis.

For annual ordinary conventions, the function of the expert panel is performed by the technical service of the Law Implementation Oversight Authority, under the conditions laid down in Article [105](#preparation-projet-budget).

The members of the panel enjoy the following guarantees:

- a term limited to the duration of the preparation of the file;

- no immediate reappointment for a related convention;

- uniform remuneration, independent of prominence or prior experience;

- possible recusal by the convention or following a public counter-expertise, for manifest conflict of interest;

- prior and public declaration of interests, funding and affiliations, added to the register of influence;

- mandatory publication of dissenting opinions, added to the file.

### Article 134 — Public counter-expertise {#contre-expertise-publique}

The file prepared by the expert panel and the first proposed list of hearings are published as soon as they are delivered.

Within a public time limit laid down by organic law, citizens, representative organisations and the future members of the convention may propose additional hearings, additional experts and supplementary notes.

The General Authority for Citizens' Conventions examines these proposals. The Civic Agenda Authority rules publicly, giving reasons, and draws up the final list of hearings, ensuring the mandatory minimum pluralism across the families of actors in the field concerned, in accordance with Article [135](#pluralite-minimale-auditions).

### Article 135 — Minimum plurality of hearings {#pluralite-minimale-auditions}

For each convention, the Civic Agenda Authority, on a proposal from the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions, draws up the list of organisations recognised as representative of the field concerned, in particular approved associations, representative trade unions, professional bodies and citizens' collectives entered in a public register.

The convention shall hear, by way of mandatory plurality, at least one representative of each of the main families of organisations in the field, in particular employers, trade unions, user associations, rights protection associations and academic experts from different schools of thought.

Beyond this minimum plurality, the convention retains the choice of its additional hearings, under the conditions laid down in Article [134](#contre-expertise-publique).

Any declared party or political group may add to the file of any convention a written contribution on the subject under deliberation. These contributions are published and entered in the public register of influence.

### Article 136 — Declarations of interests of experts {#declarations-interets-expertes-experts}

Every expert heard by a convention or by a constitutional authority shall declare in advance, publicly, their affiliations, funding, interests and potential conflicts of interest relating to the subject under consideration.

These declarations are sent to the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions and added to the public register of influence. The Authority verifies their accuracy on the basis of the information available.

The convention may, by a reasoned decision, recuse any expert whose conflicts of interest make their hearing irrelevant or suspect.

Any false declaration exposes its author to the criminal penalties laid down by law.

## Chapter IV — Protection against information manipulation

### Article 137 — Offence against popular sovereignty {#atteinte-souverainete-populaire}

Organised information manipulation aimed at distorting the conditions in which citizens' opinions are formed constitutes an offence against popular sovereignty, in particular:

- coordinated disinformation campaigns;

- the fabrication of false documents or materially false facts, knowingly disseminated for the purpose of influence;

- the use of fake accounts or automated means to artificially amplify content;

- advertising targeting that exploits personal data for political purposes outside the legal frameworks;

- the use of artificial intelligence to produce misleading content imitating real persons;

- concealed or foreign funding of an influence campaign.

Criticism of a convention, a law or an authority, even if vigorous, organised or coordinated, never constitutes information manipulation where it uses none of these methods.

### Article 138 — Preventive measures {#mesures-preventives}

The law adopted in convention lays down the precise rules on the transparency of political advertising, data targeting, content generated by artificial intelligence and recommendation algorithms.

Education in informed deliberation, the evaluation of information and the understanding of the media forms part of the constitutional missions of public education.

Digital platforms hosting a significant volume of content, as well as audiovisual and print news media with a significant audience or receiving public aid, are subject to obligations of transparency regarding their algorithms, their moderation rules, their sources of funding and their editorial practices, under the conditions laid down by law.

A public vigilance service, attached to the Independent Regulatory Authority provided for in Article [140](#autorite-regulation-vie-democratique), detects foreign digital interference. It publicly informs the conventions concerned of the campaigns it identifies. When a matter is referred by the Authority or by a convention, the judge hearing urgent applications may order, within forty-eight hours, the removal of a coordinated and inauthentic campaign targeting a convention.

### Article 139 — Sanctions {#manipulation-sanctions}

Conduct amounting to organised information manipulation constitutes offences defined by the law adopted in convention, punishable in particular by fines proportionate to the means employed, custodial sentences and additional measures including publication of the judgement.

Digital platforms and audiovisual and print news media that fail to meet their obligations of transparency, pluralism, fact-checking, distinction between news and opinion, or combating organised disinformation are sanctioned by:

- fines proportionate to turnover or audience;

- enhanced transparency measures, in particular the publication of corrections;

- in the event of repeated and characterised failures, withdrawal of the broadcasting authorisation or of the approval, for audiovisual media and services subject to approval.

Failures to meet the obligations of transparency and pluralism are sanctioned by the Independent Regulatory Authority provided for in Article [140](#autorite-regulation-vie-democratique), subject to review by the courts. Failures concerning the content of information, in particular fact-checking, the distinction between news and opinion and organised disinformation, may be sanctioned only by the ordinary courts, to which the matter is referred by the Authority or by any interested person. Withdrawal of a broadcasting authorisation or of an approval may be ordered only by a court. Where an infringement of freedom of expression is alleged, the decisions may be brought before the Constitutional Council.

The sanctions target organised and characterised conduct, not mere journalistic errors or opinions, even polemical ones.

## Chapter V — Independent Regulatory Authority for Democratic Life

### Article 140 — Principle {#autorite-regulation-vie-democratique}

An Independent Regulatory Authority for Democratic Life is established by this Constitution. It ensures the transparency, pluralism and integrity of the democratic process. To that end, it is responsible for:

- the regulation of the media and digital platforms, under the conditions laid down in this Title;

- oversight of the funding of parties and political groups, under the conditions laid down in Article [144](#financement-partis);

- oversight of campaigns in support of or opposition to referendums and of signature-collection campaigns for citizens' initiative petitions, under the conditions laid down in Article [144](#financement-partis);

- imposing the administrative sanctions provided for in Article [139](#manipulation-sanctions) and by law in the matters within its competence, and referring to the courts failures concerning the content of information.

The Authority is independent of the executive power, of citizens' conventions, of parties and political groups and of private interests. It is composed of nine members: three members of the judiciary and three persons qualified in the fields of media, digital technology or political funding, drawn by lot from public pools established according to objective criteria laid down by organic law, and three citizens drawn by lot. The term of the members drawn from the pools is six years, non-renewable, with one third renewed every two years; that of the citizens is two years. No member may have held a position of responsibility in a party, a political group or a media outlet in the preceding five years. Its powers and operating rules are laid down by organic law, in accordance with the principles of this Constitution. The integrity of its members is reviewed by the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability. They may be removed under the conditions laid down in Title [IX](#juridiction-probite).

## Chapter VI — Media pluralism

### Article 141 — Anti-concentration {#anti-concentration}

Excessive concentration of ownership of news media, whether national or transnational, is prohibited. The thresholds, criteria and prevention mechanisms are laid down by organic law.

### Article 142 — Editorial independence and public broadcasting {#independance-editoriale-audiovisuel-public}

The editorial independence of journalists is guaranteed. The law adopted in convention specifies the rights of newsrooms, in particular the conscience clause and the right of re-use.

A pluralist and independent public broadcasting service is guaranteed. Its governance is organised so as to ensure its independence from the executive power and from private interests. Its funding, expressly guaranteed by law, may not depend on short-term political decisions.

### Article 143 — Media transparency {#transparence-medias}

News media, in particular those receiving public aid, are subject to obligations of transparency regarding their governance, their funding, their shareholding and their editorial orientations, under the conditions laid down by law.

Where a media outlet attributes to a citizens' convention a decision it has not taken or inaccurate facts, the rapporteurs of that convention have a right of reply. This right covers facts only, to the exclusion of opinions and criticism.

## Chapter VII — Parties and political groups

### Article 144 — Funding of parties and campaigns {#financement-partis #campagnes-referendaires-signatures}

The funding of parties and political groups by foreign States, by foreign legal persons or legal persons under foreign control, or by natural persons who are not members of the civic body, is prohibited.

Full transparency of the sources and uses of funding of parties and political groups is guaranteed. The arrangements are laid down by law. Oversight of compliance with these rules falls to the Independent Regulatory Authority provided for in Article [140](#autorite-regulation-vie-democratique). Breaches are sanctioned under the conditions laid down by law.

Campaigns in support of or opposition to referendums, as well as signature-collection campaigns for the citizens' initiative petitions provided for by this Constitution, are subject to rules on spending caps, transparency and oversight, laid down by law and applied by the Independent Regulatory Authority provided for in Article [140](#autorite-regulation-vie-democratique).

The funding of these campaigns, and of contributions made to conventions, by the States, persons and entities mentioned in the first paragraph is prohibited.

Equality between parties and political groups in access to public media during referendum campaigns is guaranteed. The arrangements are specified by law.

### Article 145 — Dissolution of parties and political groups {#dissolution-partis}

In accordance with Article [5](#partis-groupements-politiques), which prohibits any dissolution on account of the ideas or opinions defended, the dissolution of a party or political group may be ordered only by an adversarial, reasoned and public decision of the ordinary courts, and solely in cases where the organisation or the material activities of the party undermine constitutional principles:

- the formation of combat groups, private militias or paramilitary structures;

- the organisation of, or material incitement to, acts of violence against persons, institutions or property;

- the material preparation of an attack on the inviolable rights referred to in Article [9](#droits-intangibles) or of the overthrow of the constitutional institutions;

- the existence of a systemic modus operandi established by a body of final convictions, for offences committed in the course of the party's activities, for corruption, illicit funding, fraud in petitions or referendum campaigns, organised information manipulation or coordinated harassment.

In the case provided for in the fourth indent, dissolution may be ordered only after verification of the repetition, the cumulative seriousness and the systematic nature of the breaches and of the proportionality of the dissolution. Intermediate sanctions, in particular temporary suspension of activity or deprivation of the right to take part in referendum and petition campaigns for a fixed period, may be ordered as an alternative to dissolution.

Dissolution decisions may be appealed to the Court of Cassation. They may be referred to the Constitutional Council as to their conformity with constitutional rights and freedoms.
