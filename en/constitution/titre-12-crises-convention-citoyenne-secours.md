# Title XII — Crises and the emergency convention {#crises}

## Chapter I — General principles

### Article 112 — Definition of a crisis and applicable regime {#notion-crise #architecture-regime-crise}

A crisis, within the meaning of this Title, is any serious and immediate situation that threatens the life or health of persons, the continuity of essential public services, the integrity of the territory, national security, the environment, the democratic order or the functioning of the constitutional institutions.

In the event of a crisis, two distinct mechanisms are activated simultaneously:

- immediate protective measures, which the executive administration may take within a constitutionally limited framework, subject to individualised judicial review;

- exceptional measures, which may be authorised only by the emergency convention.

### Article 113 — Absolute prohibitions {#crise-interdictions-absolues}

Even in times of crisis, any measure is absolutely prohibited whose effect or purpose is:

- to transfer effective sovereignty to the executive administration, to the army, to an administrative authority or to a restricted group;

- to suspend essential judicial guarantees;

- to deprive a person of their liberty outside the ordinary judicial framework;

- to abolish citizen oversight of crisis decisions;

- to infringe the inviolable rights guaranteed in Article [9](#droits-intangibles);

- to prolong a state of exception beyond the durations or without the controls provided for in Article [123](#secours-duree-reexamen);

- to combine with judicial power or with the review of the constitutional validity of the law.

### Article 114 — Exclusion of a military regime of exception {#exclusion-regime-militaire-exception}

No crisis situation may entail the transfer of civil powers to the military authority. The civil authority remains, in all circumstances, superior to the military authority.

The armed forces may be mobilised in support of the civil authorities under the conditions provided for by law and in Article [116](#conservatoires-cadre-interdit), under the supervision of the courts and in compliance with fundamental rights. This mobilisation confers on the military authority no power of its own of policing, administration or coercion outside the strict framework laid down by law.

No armed force or internal security force, including the gendarmerie and the police, may enter the places where the citizens' conventions, the courts and the constitutional authorities sit, or the premises of the media, or be deployed in their vicinity, without the authorisation of the institution concerned or of the emergency convention. Exceptions are the ordinary guard placed under the authority of the institution and acts ordered by a court. Any breach of this prohibition constitutes an attack on the constitutional order.

## Chapter II — Protective measures

### Article 115 — Authorised framework {#conservatoires-cadre-autorise}

The executive administration may take, without delay and under the supervision of the courts, strictly operational, necessary, proportionate and reversible measures, falling in particular within the following areas:

- the protection and rescue of persons;

- the evacuation of dangerous areas;

- the mobilisation of emergency and civil protection services;

- the securing of critical infrastructure;

- the continuity of essential public services;

- the operational coordination of security forces in compliance with their ordinary missions;

- any other strictly operational measure meeting the criteria of Article [117](#conservatoires-validite).

In the event of a proven imminent threat to national security or to the life of persons, the executive administration may also order, for a maximum non-renewable period of seventy-two hours:

1° territorially delimited restrictions on movement, distinct from the evacuation of dangerous areas;

2° the temporary closure of border crossing points.

These measures are carried out solely by the forces placed under the authority of the Minister of the Interior, within the framework of their policing missions. They are coordinated by the member who chairs the college of ministers and are subject to the judicial review provided for in Article [118](#crise-controle-juridictionnel). They automatically cease when the emergency convention takes office; the emergency convention may extend, amend or repeal them.

The measures provided for in 1° and 2° are subject to the following safeguards:

a) Jurisdiction. The Court of Public Integrity and Accountability has jurisdiction to hear cases of misconduct committed by executive officials and authorities in implementing them. A matter may be referred to it by any citizen, by the emergency convention or by the thematic oversight convention provided for in e).

b) Penalty for non-confirmation. Any measure not confirmed by the emergency convention within the time limits provided for in Article [120](#secours-saisine) is deemed unlawful. It gives rise to a right to compensation for the persons harmed and engages the personal liability of the authorities that ordered it, under the conditions laid down by organic law.

c) Annual ceiling. Recourse to these measures is limited to three episodes per calendar year. Beyond that, it requires the prior authorisation of the emergency convention ruling by qualified majority, except in a state of war declared by a thematic convention.

d) Annual audit. The Court of Public Integrity and Accountability publishes each year a report on the use of these measures: their basis, their actual duration and the outcome of the reviews. This report is transmitted to the annual ordinary convention, which may propose amendments to the regime.

e) Thematic oversight convention. Every five years, a dedicated thematic convention examines the cumulative use of these measures. It may propose any modification of the regime, including its restriction or abolition, under the conditions of constitutional amendment.

f) Review clause. The provisions relating to the measures provided for in 1° and 2°, including these safeguards, are subject to a mandatory review by a constituent convention fifteen years after the entry into force of this Constitution. Failing express reconfirmation under the amendment procedure provided for in Title [XVI](#revision), including the constituent convention and the referendum, they lapse.

### Article 116 — Prohibited framework {#conservatoires-cadre-interdit}

The following may be taken only with the express authorisation of the emergency convention:

- any restriction of the public freedoms of movement, assembly, expression, demonstration or the press beyond what is strictly necessary for immediate security;

- any house arrest or other individual restriction of the freedom to come and go, ordered under the supervision of the ordinary courts;

- any deployment on national territory of the forces placed under the authority of the Minister of Defence, beyond their ordinary missions provided for by law, and any use of military means by the gendarmerie outside its policing missions;

- any suspension or restriction of media, communication networks or the internet;

- any exceptional fiscal, budgetary or monetary measure, subject to the emergency safeguards provided for in Article [107](#depenses-cours-annee);

- any requisition of private property exceeding a threshold set by organic law;

- any modification of the powers of a constitutional authority.

The restrictions on movement and the border closures provided for in Article [115](#conservatoires-cadre-autorise) may be taken without this authorisation, solely under the conditions of that Article.

### Article 117 — Validity criteria {#conservatoires-validite}

Each protective measure shall meet the following criteria in order to be taken or maintained:

- necessity: it is indispensable to the protection of persons, property or the continuity of essential services;

- proportionality: it does not infringe rights and freedoms more than is necessary;

- reversibility: it can be lifted as soon as the crisis abates;

- time limitation: it has a fixed duration, which may be extended only after a new review;

- respect for inviolable rights: it may in no case infringe them.

### Article 118 — Judicial review and citizen remedy {#crise-controle-juridictionnel #crise-recours-citoyen}

Each protective measure is validated individually by a specialised chamber of an independent court, sitting in an emergency formation twenty-four hours a day during periods of crisis. The composition, rules of operation and time limits of this formation are laid down by organic law, in compliance with judicial independence and the right to a remedy.

Measures immediately necessary to safeguard persons may be applied without prior validation, provided that the matter is referred to the court within six hours and that the court rules within twelve hours. In the event of refusal, the measure lapses and its effects are, as far as possible, undone.

Other protective measures are subject to prior validation by the court, which rules as swiftly as possible.

The court's decisions are reasoned and public.

Any person affected by a protective measure may apply to the same court, at any time, to request the lifting, modification or restriction of that measure in its concrete application. The court rules as swiftly as possible. This right is in addition to all ordinary remedies.

## Chapter III — The emergency convention

### Article 119 — Composition and permanent standby {#secours-composition-veille}

The emergency convention is composed of one hundred and fifty citizens drawn by lot according to the representativeness criteria defined in Article [12](#composition-representativite). It is on permanent standby: its members are designated in advance, trained and placed on call for a period laid down by organic law, without any continuity of persons being ensured from one on-call period to the next. They are protected by the status of convention members defined in Article [14](#statut-conventionnel).

The emergency convention has pre-designated secondary seats, distributed across the territory and maintained in operational readiness. If the main seat is unavailable or a physical meeting is impossible, it sits in a secondary seat or by secure means of communication, according to protocols approved by the Constitutional Council. Its means of communication are independent of ordinary networks. A validly constituted convention is binding on the public authorities wherever it meets. Members prevented from sitting are replaced without delay by substitutes. The quorum and majorities are always calculated on the full membership of the convention, which never deliberates with fewer than half of its members. Only a meeting convened according to these protocols and whose attendance list is published is valid. If several meetings claim to be the convention, the Constitutional Council designates the legitimate meeting; if the Council is prevented from doing so, the meeting with the most regularly designated members prevails. Any other meeting is automatically null and void, under the conditions of Article [7](#tyrannie-majorite-resistance).

### Article 120 — Referral and time limit for sitting {#secours-saisine #secours-delai-siege}

The emergency convention may take up a matter on its own initiative as soon as a protective measure has been validated by the court. The executive administration and the court are under a constitutional obligation to inform it without delay of any protective measure in force or requested.

Failing self-referral within twenty-four hours, the General Authority for Citizens' Conventions automatically convenes it.

In the absence of a protective measure, a matter may also be referred to the emergency convention by the concurring request of at least two distinct constitutional authorities, for the purpose of examining a political, institutional or systemic crisis.

In addition, matters are referred to it only in the following cases:

- by the Civic Agenda Authority, for the re-examination of a suspended text subject to a legal deadline, under the conditions provided for in Article [23](#reexamen-cas-suspension);
- by at least one third of the ministers, in the event of persistent deadlock within the college of ministers, under the conditions provided for in Article [52](#structure-collegiale);
- automatically, when a ministerial vacancy is not filled within the period provided for in Article [57](#interim-ministeriel);
- by any external relations representative, in the event of an international emergency, under the conditions provided for in Article [58](#representants-relations-exterieures);
- by the Minister of Defence, who informs it of any defensive or urgent operation, and for the re-examination of military operations, under the conditions provided for in Article [61](#autorisation-operations-militaires);
- by the Standing Commission for the Oversight of Intelligence and the Armed Forces, in the event of a suspected attack on the constitutional order, under the conditions provided for in Article [69](#controle-renseignement-armees);
- automatically, in the event of failure of the second examination of the budget, under the conditions provided for in Article [109](#blocage-adoption-budget).

Referrals for the re-examination of a suspended text, for deadlock within the college of ministers, for a ministerial vacancy and for failure of the budget do not constitute a crisis within the meaning of Article [112](#notion-crise). In these cases the convention rules only on the subject of the referral and may not take any of the measures provided for in Article [116](#conservatoires-cadre-interdit).

The emergency convention sits no later than seventy-two hours after the referral.

### Article 121 — Powers {#secours-competences}

The emergency convention:

- authorises, by qualified majority, the exceptional measures falling within the prohibited framework defined in Article [116](#conservatoires-cadre-interdit);

- regulates their duration, scope and conditions;

- may at any time suspend or modify them or order them to be lifted;

- ratifies, in the event of an international emergency, the major treaties falling under Article [147](#traites-ratification), subject to confirmation by the annual ordinary convention;

- authorises the use of the nuclear force under the conditions provided for in Article [62](#emploi-arme-nucleaire);

- authorises military operations when urgency does not allow a dedicated thematic convention to be convened, and confirms or terminates the urgent operations provided for in Article [61](#autorisation-operations-militaires);

- may request the removal of any member of the executive administration who has acted outside the framework, in accordance with the procedures of Title [IX](#juridiction-probite);

- may, by qualified majority, suspend certain ordinary conventions on grounds of operational priority, by reasoned decision.

### Article 122 — Oversight and continuity maintained {#secours-controles-maintenus #secours-continuite-conventions}

The exceptional measures authorised by the emergency convention are subject to the constitutional and scientific review applicable to ordinary laws, according to procedures adapted to the urgency and specified by organic law. The Constitutional Council, the Law Implementation Oversight Authority, the Court of Public Integrity and Accountability and the competent courts retain full jurisdiction during the crisis.

Ordinary conventions in progress continue their work during the crisis, except where the emergency convention takes a reasoned decision of suspension by qualified majority. Institutional continuity is the principle; suspension, the reasoned exception.

### Article 123 — Duration, re-examination and deactivation {#secours-duree-reexamen #secours-desactivation}

The emergency convention lasts as long as the crisis lasts. Every thirty days, it shall vote by qualified majority on maintaining the state of crisis and the exceptional measures in force. Failing confirmation, the exceptional measures lapse.

The total duration of the state of crisis may not exceed six months. Beyond that period, the emergency convention is deactivated and the crisis measures that need to be maintained are submitted to a dedicated thematic convention, deliberating according to the ordinary procedures. Organic law lays down the arrangements for this transition.

By way of exception, in the event of armed conflict or of a disaster of such magnitude that a return to the ordinary regime within the six-month period is impossible, the emergency convention may, by a majority of three quarters of its members, extend the state of crisis for an additional period of three months, renewable under the same conditions. The matter is automatically referred to the Constitutional Council at each extension, and the Council may find, by reasoned decision, that the conditions justifying the exception have ended.

The emergency convention may deactivate itself by a qualified majority vote finding that the conditions of crisis have ended. It is also deactivated when the Constitutional Council finds, by reasoned decision, that the conditions of crisis have ended. A matter may be referred to the Council for this purpose by the Civic Agenda Authority, by the Defender of Rights, by one fifth of the members of the emergency convention, or by a petition gathering one per cent of the civic body.
