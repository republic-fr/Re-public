<p class="lead">Sortition raises serious objections. Here are the most common ones, and how the text answers them. Have another one? <a href="/en/comment/">Ask it</a>.</p>

## <span class="kicker">01</span>Competence and legitimacy

### Citizens drawn by lot are not competent to make the law.

Competence is not assumed: it is organised. Every convention receives training, a briefing file presenting opposing views, pluralist expertise, public hearings, drafting assistance and the time it needs (Articles [15](#conditions-materielles-deliberation) and [21](#thematique-cycle-deliberatif)). This is what the Citizens’ Convention for Climate showed: 150 ordinary people, well equipped, produced 149 precise proposals. Elected representatives are not experts in everything either: they rely on support staff and hearings. The text gives these resources directly to citizens.

### Without elections, where does legitimacy come from?

First from representativeness: a convention drawn by lot according to sex, age, qualifications, social background and region (Article [12](#composition-representativite)) looks far more like the country than an elected assembly does. Then from procedure: law is legitimate only if it results from informed and pluralist deliberation (Preamble). Finally from the people themselves: every amendment of the Constitution is put to a referendum (Article [168](#referendum-revision-majorite)).

### 150 people cannot represent 68 million inhabitants.

They do not claim to speak on behalf of each person, as an elected representative does. They decide in place of everyone, just as the jurors of a French criminal court (cour d’assises) give judgement in the name of the French people. Their legitimacy rests not on a mandate but on equality: every citizen has the same chance of being drawn by lot, and the convention includes, in the same proportions as the country, women and men, young and old, manual workers and managers, people with and without qualifications, town and country dwellers (Article [12](#composition-representativite)). No elected assembly achieves this.

Above all, a convention never decides alone, nor for ever. There are several each year, with new members every time. Every law is reviewed by the Constitutional Council before it enters into force (Article [39](#controle-prealable-automatique-lois)), may then be challenged by any person (Article [40](#cc-saisine-posteriori)) and may be amended or repealed by a later convention. The Constitution itself can be changed only by referendum (Article [168](#referendum-revision-majorite)).

### Who is accountable, if no one is re-elected?

Conventions are temporary and there is no re-election: no one has a career to protect or a clientele to satisfy. Those who hold lasting power, ministers and public office-holders, are continuously overseen by the Law Implementation Oversight Authority (Title [VIII](#controle-execution)) and can be removed by the Court of Public Integrity (Title [IX](#juridiction-probite)). A minister who fails to implement the laws can also be warned, then replaced (Article [56](#duree-mandat-ministeriel)).

### With so much judicial review, does the project not risk government by judges?

The members of the Constitutional Council and of the Court of Public Integrity, and the non-judicial members of the High Council of the Judiciary, are drawn by lot: no political power chooses them (Articles [36](#cc-composition), [78](#juridiction-probite-composition) and [92](#csm-designation-membres)). Their terms are single and non-renewable, and their decisions are reasoned, public and accompanied by dissenting opinions (Article [44](#cc-autorite-decisions)). The Constitutional Council never rewrites a law: it suspends it and sends it back to a citizens’ convention, which may correct or confirm it. Only violations of absolute rights, such as the prohibition of torture, and of the rules that organise the institutions cannot be confirmed (Article [23](#reexamen-cas-suspension)). And the people keep the last word on the Constitution itself, through the constituent convention and the referendum (Title [XVI](#revision)).

## <span class="kicker">02</span>Risks

### Lobbies will manipulate inexperienced citizens.

The text devotes an entire title to this (Title [XIV](#probite-deliberative)). All influence shall be declared and published in a public register (Articles [129](#probite-deliberation-citoyenne) and [130](#registre-public-influences)). Experts heard by conventions declare their interests (Article [136](#declarations-interets-expertes-experts)), pluralism in hearings is mandatory (Article [135](#pluralite-minimale-auditions)), and any pressure on a convention member is an aggravated offence (Article [131](#influences-interdictions-sanctions)). A career politician is, moreover, a longer-lasting target than a citizen who sits for three months.

### Who guarantees that the draw is not rigged?

The draw is public and verifiable. The algorithm is published, and the draw starts from a public source of randomness, known only at the moment of the draw: no one can choose the result in advance. The Law Implementation Oversight Authority re-runs the calculation for every draw, and any draw can be challenged before the Constitutional Council within fifteen days (Article [13](#eligibilite-participation)).

### Experts and ‘scientific review’ will govern instead of the people.

No. Scientific review covers only a manifest scientific error that threatens health, personal integrity or the environment, and only where there is a scientific consensus (Article [48](#controle-scientifique-champ)). It can only suspend a text, never rewrite it, and does not judge its political merits (Article [49](#controle-scientifique-suspension)). On the economic and social effects of a law, it only gives a public opinion, without being able to suspend it: the convention decides with full knowledge of the facts (Article [48](#controle-scientifique-champ)). The last word belongs to a citizens’ convention (Article [23](#reexamen-cas-suspension)).

### Does power not simply pass to experts and the administration, in a disguised oligarchy?

No one chooses the experts: they are drawn by lot from open pools, according to public pluralism criteria, overseen by an authority itself made up of citizens drawn by lot (Articles [31](#apc-missions), [132](#vivier-public-experts) and [133](#jury-experts-independants)). The briefing file prepared by the experts is published and subject to public counter-expertise (Article [134](#contre-expertise-publique)). Experts declare their interests, are paid at a uniform rate and can be challenged and excluded by the convention (Articles [133](#jury-experts-independants) and [136](#declarations-interets-expertes-experts)). The draft State budget, prepared by the administration, is sent to the annual ordinary convention together with an independent report by the Law Implementation Oversight Authority, which flags its areas of opacity and insufficiently justified spending (Article [105](#preparation-projet-budget)).

### Could a foreign power manipulate the conventions?

The text deprives it of its main levers. All foreign funding of parties, petitions, referendum campaigns and contributions to conventions is prohibited (Article [144](#financement-partis)). A monitoring service detects online interference campaigns and publicly alerts the conventions; a judge can order their removal within 48 hours (Article [138](#mesures-preventives)). Every convention member may keep their identity confidential, which is the rule for defence matters, and revealing it is an aggravated offence (Articles [14](#statut-conventionnel) and [131](#influences-interdictions-sanctions)). A member who is threatened may withdraw and be replaced by a substitute drawn by lot (Article [13](#eligibilite-participation)). Finally, referendums are voted on paper (Article [167](#referendum-revision-principe)).

### A majority drawn by lot could oppress a minority.

Fundamental rights are inviolable: no law, no crisis and no amendment may infringe them (Articles [7](#tyrannie-majorite-resistance) and [9](#droits-intangibles)). Every law is reviewed by the Constitutional Council before it comes into force (Article [39](#controle-prealable-automatique-lois)).

### Does procedural democracy not come at the cost of freedom of expression and pluralism?

Parties remain free and may not be dissolved for their ideas (Articles [5](#partis-groupements-politiques) and [145](#dissolution-partis)). The integrity check on candidates concerns only acts, such as violence or a conviction for an attack on the institutions, never opinions (Article [80](#controle-prealable-probite)). Penalties for information manipulation target organised conduct, never opinions or journalistic errors; any penalty linked to the content of information, such as the withdrawal of a broadcasting frequency, is imposed by a judge (Article [139](#manipulation-sanctions)). Convention hearings shall represent all the main groups of stakeholders (Article [135](#pluralite-minimale-auditions)).

## <span class="kicker">03</span>Effectiveness and continuity

### Without a President, who decides in a crisis?

An emergency convention, drawn by lot in advance and permanently on standby, can meet in less than 72 hours (Articles [119](#secours-composition-veille) and [120](#secours-saisine)). In the meantime, the administration takes immediate protective measures, coordinated by the minister who chairs the college of ministers that year, under the oversight of a judge (Article [52](#structure-collegiale) and Articles [115](#conservatoires-cadre-autorise) to [118](#crise-controle-juridictionnel)). The emergency convention is limited in time and may never touch inviolable rights (Articles [113](#crise-interdictions-absolues) and [123](#secours-duree-reexamen)).

### Does the crisis regime not leave France vulnerable to a cyberattack, a pandemic or a war?

The administration acts immediately, without waiting for any convention, to protect people and infrastructure, under the oversight of a judge who rules within a few hours (Articles [115](#conservatoires-cadre-autorise) and [118](#crise-controle-juridictionnel)). The Minister of Defence may order without delay a response to armed aggression, but also an evacuation of nationals, a hostage rescue or assistance to an ally under attack, which the emergency convention confirms or halts within 72 hours; a long war falls under a defence regime voted for one year, renewable (Article [61](#autorisation-operations-militaires)). The emergency convention is appointed and trained in advance, has fallback sites and can sit remotely (Article [119](#secours-composition-veille)). In the event of armed conflict, the state of crisis may be extended beyond six months (Article [123](#secours-duree-reexamen)). The only absolute prohibitions concern inviolable rights and the seizure of power (Article [113](#crise-interdictions-absolues)).

### Could a crisis regime not be used for a coup?

Most modern coups began with emergency powers. The text closes that door. During the first hours, the administration may close borders or restrict movement using its police forces, under the oversight of a judge (Article [115](#conservatoires-cadre-autorise)); any deployment of the army on national territory requires the agreement of the emergency convention (Article [116](#conservatoires-cadre-interdit)). No armed or police force, apart from the ordinary guard of the institutions, may approach the institutions or the media without that agreement (Article [114](#exclusion-regime-militaire-exception)). If members of the emergency convention are prevented from sitting, their substitutes replace them: a handful of compliant members gathered on the sidelines can never speak in its name (Article [119](#secours-composition-veille)). Every member of the armed forces shall refuse an order contrary to the Constitution, obedience excuses nothing, and the acts of a power installed by force are void (Article [7](#tyrannie-majorite-resistance)). Finally, all troop movements are reported in real time to an oversight commission (Article [69](#controle-renseignement-armees)).

### Will layer upon layer of review not paralyse decision-making?

Scientific review is narrow: it applies only in the case of a manifest scientific error threatening health or the environment (Article [48](#controle-scientifique-champ)). Scientific and constitutional review take place at the same time, within one month each, extendable to three months at most; once that period has passed, the law comes into force and can still be challenged before the Council (Article [39](#controle-prealable-automatique-lois)). A challenge to a law already in force does not suspend it: the Constitutional Council itself determines the effects of its decision over time (Article [43](#effet-controle-prealable)). Laws, the budget and appointments are adopted by an absolute majority of the convention’s members: a minority cannot block everything. Two thirds are required only for exceptional decisions, such as crisis measures (Article [17](#majorite-qualifiee)). Referendums are reserved for amendments of the Constitution and major European decisions. Emergencies fall to the emergency convention, which sits within 72 hours (Article [120](#secours-saisine)).

### Without a head of state, who speaks for France at the European Council or the G7?

The minister who chairs the college of ministers, in turn for one year, sits on the European Council and at summits, on the basis of a written and public mandate from the college, with no powers of their own (Article [52](#structure-collegiale)). This is the Swiss model: seven equal ministers, a rotating presidency, and the same person to represent the country. Day-to-day negotiations are entrusted to specialised representatives, who negotiate on the basis of written and public mandates from the competent ministers (Articles [58](#representants-relations-exterieures), [59](#representants-transparence-abstention) and [146](#traites-negociation-conclusion)). In an international emergency, the emergency convention may ratify a treaty, subject to confirmation (Article [147](#traites-ratification)).

### With short terms and short-lived conventions, how can a policy be pursued over twenty or thirty years?

Continuity does not rest on convention members, but on several lasting elements: an agenda-setting convention sets priorities and budget trajectories over five years (Articles [19](#convention-programmation) and [103](#budget-convention-programmation)); the rapporteurs of each convention preserve its memory for five to ten years and are heard by the following ones (Article [20](#thematique-formation-objet)); the oversight and judicial authorities have terms of six to nine years, renewed by thirds or by halves to ensure continuity (Articles [37](#cc-duree-mandat), [79](#juridiction-probite-duree-mandat) and [93](#csm-duree-mandat)). The administration, for its part, is permanent.

## <span class="kicker">04</span>Everyday life

### I don’t want to be drawn by lot.

Sitting is a civic duty, like serving on a jury in a criminal court. You can be excused on serious grounds, in particular medical or family reasons (Article [13](#eligibilite-participation)). During the convention, your income and your job are guaranteed, and your expenses and childcare are covered (Article [14](#statut-conventionnel)). At national level, around a thousand people sit each year.

### What about political parties? Will I no longer be able to vote for my ideas?

Parties remain free, and all opinions are protected (Article [5](#partis-groupements-politiques)). They continue to debate, propose, mobilise public opinion and launch petitions, and may submit a written contribution, which is published, to the briefing file of each convention (Article [135](#pluralite-minimale-auditions)). What changes is that they no longer choose those who make the law. You keep the vote on the Constitution, the petition (Article [33](#seuils-effets-petitions)) and the right to challenge a law (Article [40](#cc-saisine-posteriori)).

### Will foreign nationals be able to vote or sit in a convention?

Yes, after five years of lawful residence in France. The sovereign people is the civic body: adult French nationals, and adult foreign nationals who have lived in France for at least five years (Article [13](#eligibilite-participation)). All of them can sign petitions, vote in referendums and be drawn by lot. Those who live under the law over the long term take part in making it. New Zealand has done the same since 1975: permanent residents there vote in national elections and referendums.

### How much does it cost?

About half as much as the current system: €1.7bn a year, against €3.7bn for the presidency, Parliament, elections and local councillors’ allowances. Even under the most expensive assumptions, the reform still costs around €1.2bn a year less. The details, sources and budget model are on the [How](/en/how/) page.

## <span class="kicker">05</span>The project

### This Constitution carries no values and no progress on rights.

That is a deliberate choice. This text proposes a political system: who decides, how, and subject to what checks. It does not settle in advance the major choices facing society, which belong to citizens themselves.

Values and rights will be written by a fundamental rights convention: a hundred and fifty citizens drawn by lot will draft the Declaration of Rights annexed to the Constitution, starting from the 1789 Declaration, the 1946 Preamble and the Charter for the Environment (Article [8](#declaration-droits-fondamentaux)). The draft itself will then be amended by the constituent convention, and put to a referendum (Title [XVI](#revision)).

The text only lays down a foundation that no one will be able to call into question: dignity, the prohibition of torture and of the death penalty, equality, freedom of conscience, the rights of the defence (Article [9](#droits-intangibles)). For the rest, what we propose is not a party programme: it is a method for ensuring that these choices are made by citizens.

### Does this already exist anywhere?

Not on the scale of an entire country, but democratic sortition has proved itself. In Ireland, assemblies made up mostly or entirely of citizens drawn by lot prepared the 2015 and 2018 referendums on same-sex marriage and abortion. In German-speaking Belgium, a permanent citizens’ council drawn by lot has, since 2019, organised assemblies whose recommendations the parliament must consider. In France, the Citizens’ Convention for Climate (2019–2020) and the one on end of life (2022–2023) showed the quality of these deliberations. This project goes further: it gives the conventions decision-making power.

### How do we get from the Fifth Republic to this system?

Democratically: a law passed under the current rules opens the process; a constituent convention of three hundred citizens drawn by lot and a fundamental rights convention sit in parallel; a referendum ratifies both texts. Everything is prepared during the campaign, so that the new institutions are in place within a month of ratification (Title [XVII](#transition)). The steps and the two possible legal routes are detailed on the [How](/en/how/) page.

### Is the text final?

No. It is a working draft, open to all. Every article can be commented on and every change proposed. The substantive questions currently under discussion are listed and debated publicly on [GitHub](https://github.com/{{repo}}/issues).
